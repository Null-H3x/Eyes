# Field Report 89 — SWEEPS ARE NOT FUTILE: THEY TEST A DIFFERENT HYPOTHESIS

*Instrument: `eyekey` (keyword-derived alphabets). July 2026.*
*Cycle: EYESPIRAL. Negatives and self-corrections first.*

---

## 0. SELF-CORRECTION — I overstated the consequence of FR81

Closing the previous cycle I wrote:

> *"If the corpus sits below its unicity distance, no generator sweep can succeed,
> because the answer isn't determined by the ciphertext at all."*

**That is wrong, and the error matters because it would have ended a productive line
of work.**

The unicity argument concerns determining the key from **plaintext redundancy**, by
likelihood: with 16.3 bits of redundancy against a 33.59-bit residual, no
likelihood-based cryptanalysis distinguishes the candidates.

A generator sweep uses **none of that**. It tests candidates against the skeleton — a
**hard constraint** at 83⁻³⁷⁸ selectivity, derived from isomorph and depth structure,
not from plaintext statistics. If the true `C` lies in a swept family, the filter
identifies it uniquely **regardless of unicity distance**.

The two attacks are independent:

| attack | uses | limited by |
|---|---|---|
| likelihood / redundancy | plaintext statistics | **unicity distance** — corpus is 51× too short |
| generator sweep | skeleton hard constraint | **whether `C` is low-complexity** |

So 1.36 billion nulls are not evidence of futility. They are evidence about **`C`'s
construction**, and a different question entirely.

## 1. The gap this exposed

Reframed as *"is `C` low-complexity?"*, the coverage question becomes precise. Swept
so far: PRNG shuffles, algebraic maps, digit-derived orderings, in-game **list
orderings**, MAGICK variants, the posited alphabet, calendar derivations, and the
entire Finnish lexicon as keywords.

**Never swept: in-game and authored text as keywords.** The puzzle sits inside a game
full of authored material — material names, spell identifiers, book text, the English
secret messages, the Emerald Tablet phrase — and none of it has been tried as a key.

That is a natural construction for this author and it was missing.

## 3. A claim that would not verify

The community records `0xacf68674`, one of the byte sequences the messages are stored
under in the EXE, as the **CRC-32b of "lumikki"** (Finnish: Snow White). It motivated
the Finnish-lexicon sweep.

Tested across **60 variants** — case, null-termination, UTF-8 and UTF-16 in both
endiannesses, byte-swap, bit-reversal, XOR-out. **None match.**

Either the reported hex is inexact, a nonstandard polynomial or initial value was
used, or the hashed input includes surrounding bytes. **The claim is unverified and
should not be leaned on** until someone reproduces it against the actual binary. The
sweeps it motivated stand on their own merits regardless.

---

## 4. What the cumulative null now means

**1.36 billion candidates, zero survivors**, spanning every construction I can
motivate: random (PRNG), algebraic, digit-structural, list-derived, calendar-derived,
and lexical in two languages plus the game's own vocabulary.

Read correctly, that is a statement about **description complexity**:

> If `C` were generated by any construction a person would plausibly write down, the
> skeleton filter would almost certainly have found it. It has not. **`C` is most
> likely an arbitrary permutation** — hand-shuffled, physically shuffled, or drawn
> from a source outside anything reachable.

That conclusion is independent of FR81's unicity result, and the two compose badly for
the puzzle: **an arbitrary `C` is unreachable by sweep, and a corpus below its unicity
distance is unreachable by likelihood.** Both routes require external evidence, for
different reasons.
